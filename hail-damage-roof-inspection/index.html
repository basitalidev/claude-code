Why Denver Property Owners Choose Our Hail Damage Roof Inspection

Hail is the most common — and most expensive — cause of roof damage along Colorado's Front Range. The Denver metro typically sees severe hail between April and September, and a single storm can damage thousands of roofs across multiple suburbs in an afternoon.

The trouble is that hail damage is rarely obvious from the ground. A shingle can look fine from your driveway while its fiberglass mat is fractured underneath, setting the stage for leaks and granule loss months later. A professional hail damage roof inspection documents what's really there, so you can file with confidence — or skip a claim you don't need.

When to schedule a hail damage roof inspection

  • Hail about the size of a quarter or larger fell in your area
  • You see fresh dents on gutters, downspouts, mailboxes or vehicles
  • Neighbors are getting adjuster visits or new roofs
  • Granules are washing out of downspouts after a storm
  • You're not sure whether damage is new or from an earlier storm

How Hail Damages a Roof

When a hailstone strikes an asphalt shingle, it can knock away the protective granules, crush the asphalt layer, and crack the fiberglass mat beneath. The damaged spot — often called a bruise — may feel soft to the touch and darken over time as UV exposure attacks the exposed asphalt. Within a season or two, those bruises can become cracks and leak points.

Hail also dents metal roof components, splits aging cedar shakes, cracks clay and concrete tile, and punctures some flat-roof membranes. Wind-driven hail can damage one or two slopes heavily while leaving others untouched, which is why we inspect and document every slope individually.

Hail Damage vs. Normal Wear

Insurance companies pay for sudden, accidental storm damage — not for age-related wear, manufacturing defects, blistering or foot traffic. Part of our job is distinguishing true hail impacts from look-alikes, so your claim is built on damage an adjuster will recognize. If what we find isn't hail damage, we'll tell you, which can save you from a denied claim on your record.

After the Storm: Steps to Protect Your Claim

  1. Note the date and time of the storm and photograph hail on the ground if it's safe to do so.
  2. Schedule a roof inspection promptly, while evidence is fresh.
  3. Review your policy for your deductible and reporting requirements.
  4. File your claim with your insurer if damage is confirmed, and share our photo report.
  5. Be wary of storm chasers who go door-to-door after hail — work with established, local professionals.

FAQ

Hail Damage Roof Inspection FAQ

What size hail damages a roof?

Hail around one inch in diameter (quarter-sized) and larger commonly damages asphalt shingles, though older or brittle roofs can be damaged by smaller hail, and wind-driven hail does more damage than hail that falls straight down.

How long do I have to file a hail claim in Colorado?

Colorado law generally gives homeowners at least one year from the date of the storm to file a hail or wind claim, but your policy may have its own notice requirements. Get your roof inspected promptly and check your policy or ask your agent.

Will filing a hail claim raise my insurance rates?

Hail is generally treated as a weather-related, no-fault event, but every insurer is different. An inspection first helps ensure you only file when real damage is present.

Can you inspect for hail damage on tile or metal roofs?

Yes. We document cracked or broken tile, dented metal panels and damaged trim, using drone imagery where walking the roof could cause additional breakage.
