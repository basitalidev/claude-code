Local roofing conditions

What Boulder Roofs Are Up Against

Boulder's powerful winter and spring downslope windstorms are notorious for stripping shingles, ridge caps and flashing. The city also experiences hail, heavy snow and intense high-altitude UV. Historic homes in Mapleton Hill and Whittier often have slate, wood or complex roofs requiring careful inspection, while many mid-century homes in Table Mesa and Martin Acres have low-slope or flat sections. Wildfire risk in the wildland-urban interface is also a growing consideration for roofing materials.

How much does a roof inspection cost in Boulder?

Pricing depends on the size, pitch and material of your roof and the type of inspection you need. Call or request an inspection online and we'll give you a clear, upfront price for your Boulder property before we schedule. There are no hidden fees.

How quickly can you inspect my roof in Boulder?

We typically schedule roof inspections in Boulder within 24–48 hours, with priority scheduling for real estate deadlines and after major hailstorms. Your photo report usually arrives the same day or next business day.

How often should Boulder roofs be inspected?

Annually at minimum, and after any major wind event. Boulder's windstorms can damage roofs without obvious signs from the ground.

Do you inspect for wildfire-related roofing concerns?

Our inspections note roofing material type and conditions like debris buildup in gutters and valleys, which are relevant to wildfire preparedness.

What should I do after a hailstorm in Boulder?

Note the date of the storm, look for dents on gutters, vents and vehicles, and schedule a hail damage roof inspection while the evidence is fresh. If damage is confirmed, our report helps you file a well-documented insurance claim.
