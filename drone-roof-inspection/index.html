Why Denver Property Owners Choose Our Drone Roof Inspection

Clay and concrete tile, natural slate, very steep pitches and multi-story homes are common across the Denver metro, from historic Capitol Hill homes to the estates of Cherry Hills Village and Castle Pines. Walking these roofs can crack tiles or put an inspector at serious risk.

Drone roof inspections solve both problems. Our aerial imaging captures every slope, ridge and penetration in detail, and we combine it with ground and ladder observations so you still get a complete, specialist-level assessment.

When to schedule a drone roof inspection

  • Your roof is tile, slate or another fragile material
  • The roof is very steep or three or more stories high
  • The roof is wet, snowy or icy
  • You need a fast overview of a large or complex roof

Drone Inspections vs. Walking the Roof

Drones are a powerful tool, but they don't replace experience. Some damage — like soft hail bruising on asphalt shingles — is best confirmed by hand. That's why we walk roofs when it's safe and appropriate, and use drones where they offer better safety or coverage. Many inspections use both.

FAQ

Drone Roof Inspection FAQ

Is a drone inspection as thorough as walking the roof?

For many roof types, yes. For asphalt shingles with suspected hail bruising, we combine drone imagery with hands-on inspection when safe to give you the most accurate assessment.

Are there places in Denver where you can't fly a drone?

Some areas near airports and certain facilities have airspace restrictions. Where flying isn't permitted, we use ladder-based inspection and other methods.

Will I get to see the drone photos?

Yes. Key aerial images are included in your report, labeled to show exactly where each finding is located.
