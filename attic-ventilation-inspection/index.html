Why Denver Property Owners Choose Our Attic & Ventilation Inspection

Your attic works together with your roof. In summer, poor ventilation traps heat that can bake shingles from below. In winter, warm, moist air escaping from the living space can condense on cold decking, feed mold, and melt rooftop snow that refreezes at the eaves as ice dams.

Denver's wide temperature swings — sometimes 40 degrees or more in a day — make attic performance especially important. Our attic and ventilation inspections identify the root causes of these problems so they can be corrected rather than repeatedly patched.

When to schedule a attic & ventilation inspection

  • You've had ice dams or icicles along the eaves
  • You see frost or moisture on attic nails or decking
  • Upstairs rooms are hard to heat or cool
  • Your shingles are aging faster than expected
  • There's a musty smell or visible mold in the attic

How Ice Dams Form

Heat escaping into the attic warms the roof deck and melts snow. Meltwater runs down to the colder eaves and refreezes, building a ridge of ice. Water then pools behind the dam and can back up under shingles into the walls and ceilings. The long-term fix is usually better air sealing, insulation and ventilation — not just heat cables.

FAQ

Attic & Ventilation Inspection FAQ

Can too much ventilation be a problem?

Yes. Unbalanced ventilation — such as mixing different exhaust types or lacking adequate intake — can pull conditioned air or weather into the attic. Balance matters more than quantity.

Are bathroom fans supposed to vent into the attic?

No. Bath and kitchen fans should exhaust to the exterior. Venting into the attic is a common cause of condensation and mold.

Will fixing ventilation extend my roof's life?

Proper ventilation helps shingles perform as designed and is often required to maintain manufacturer warranties.
