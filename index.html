Hit by Hail? Get Your Roof Inspected Before You File a Claim.

The Denver metro sees severe hail most often between April and September. Even one-inch hail can fracture shingles and knock away protective granules, often without any sign from the ground.

Our hail inspections use test squares, impact mapping and date-stamped photos to give you an honest answer to the question every homeowner asks after a storm: do I actually have damage worth claiming?

Signs you need an inspection after a storm

Roof Inspection Basics

What is included in a roof inspection?

Our standard roof inspection covers the roof covering (shingles, tile, metal or membrane), flashing, valleys, ridge caps, pipe boots and penetrations, vents, skylights, gutters, drip edge, fascia and soffits, decking, and — where accessible — the attic, ventilation and insulation. You receive a written report with labeled photos, condition ratings, an estimate of remaining roof life and clear recommendations.

How long does a roof inspection take?

Most residential roof inspections in Denver take 45 to 90 minutes on site. Larger, steeper or multi-level homes, tile roofs, and commercial properties can take longer. Your photo report is usually delivered the same day or by the next business day.

How much does a roof inspection cost in Denver?

Cost depends on the size, pitch and material of the roof and the type of inspection — a storm damage check, a real estate inspection, a roof certification and a commercial inspection each involve different levels of work and documentation. Call or request an inspection online and we'll give you a clear, upfront price before we schedule. There are never hidden fees.

Do I need to be home during the roof inspection?

Not necessarily. We can inspect the exterior roof without you there, as long as we have safe access. If you'd like us to inspect the attic or check an interior leak, someone will need to let us in. Many clients like to be present at the end so we can walk through our findings together.

Will walking on my roof damage it?

Our inspectors are trained to walk roofs safely using soft-soled footwear and proper technique. For roofs that shouldn't be walked — such as clay or concrete tile, slate, very steep pitches, or roofs that are wet or icy — we use drone imaging and ladder-based inspection instead, so your roof is never put at risk.

What's the difference between a roof inspection and a home inspection?

A general home inspector evaluates the entire house and often views the roof only from the ground, a ladder or a window. A dedicated roof inspection is performed by a roofing specialist who walks the roof (when safe), examines individual components up close, identifies hail and wind damage, and estimates remaining service life. Many Denver home inspectors recommend a specialist roof inspection when they see potential concerns.

Hail, Storms & Insurance

When is hail season in Denver?

Hail season along Colorado's Front Range generally runs from April through September, with the most active stretch typically from May through July. Severe storms can still occur outside those months, so it's worth having your roof checked any time hail or high winds pass through your neighborhood.

How do I know if my roof has hail damage?

From the ground, look for dents in gutters, downspouts, metal vents, window screens or AC units, and for shingle granules washing out of your downspouts. On the roof itself, hail damage appears as dark spots where granules have been knocked loose, soft "bruises" in the shingle, cracks or exposed fiberglass mat. Because much of this can't be seen from the ground, a professional hail damage roof inspection is the most reliable way to know.

Should I get a roof inspection before filing an insurance claim?

Yes — we recommend it. An inspection tells you whether you have damage that's likely to meet your policy's threshold before a claim goes on your record. If damage is present, our photo documentation helps you file a well-supported claim and gives you a reference point when the insurance adjuster inspects your roof.

How long do I have to file a hail damage claim in Colorado?

Colorado law generally gives homeowners at least one year from the date of a hail or wind storm to file a claim, but individual policies can include their own notice requirements. Check your policy or ask your agent, and get your roof inspected soon after the storm — evidence is easiest to document while it's fresh.

Can you meet the insurance adjuster at my property?

In many cases, yes. Having someone familiar with your roof's documented damage present during the adjuster's visit can help make sure every damaged slope and collateral item is seen. Ask about adjuster meetings when you schedule.

What are Class 4 impact-resistant shingles, and are they worth it in Denver?

Class 4 shingles earn the highest impact rating under the UL 2218 test standard, meaning they resisted cracking when struck by a 2-inch steel ball. In hail-prone Denver they can significantly reduce future damage, and many Colorado insurers offer premium discounts for them. During your inspection we can confirm whether your existing roof uses impact-resistant shingles.

Real Estate & Roof Certifications

Should I get a roof inspection when buying a house in Denver?

Absolutely. Roof replacement is one of the most expensive surprises a new homeowner can face, and Denver's hail history means many roofs have hidden damage. A specialist roof inspection during your inspection period gives you documented findings you can use to request repairs, a seller credit or a price reduction.

What is a roof certification?

A roof certification is a written statement from a roofing professional that, based on an inspection, the roof is in serviceable condition and expected to last for a stated period. Lenders, insurers and buyers sometimes request one. If the roof doesn't qualify, we'll explain what repairs would be needed for it to be certified.

How quickly can I get a roof inspection during a real estate transaction?

We know inspection objection deadlines are tight. We typically schedule real estate roof inspections within 24–48 hours across the Denver metro and deliver the report the same day or next business day.

Roof Lifespan & Maintenance

How often should I have my roof inspected in Colorado?

We recommend a professional roof inspection at least once a year — ideally in late summer or early fall after hail season — and after any significant hail or wind event. Older roofs (15+ years) and roofs with a history of leaks benefit from more frequent checks.

How long does a roof last in Denver?

Lifespan depends on material, installation quality, ventilation and storm exposure. As a general guide in Denver's climate, asphalt shingle roofs often last about 15–25 years, metal roofs 40 years or more, and concrete or clay tile and slate can last 50 years or longer. Intense UV and repeated hail can shorten those ranges, which is why regular inspections matter.

What causes ice dams, and can an inspection prevent them?

Ice dams form when warm air escaping into the attic melts snow on the roof, which then refreezes at the colder eaves. Water backs up behind the ice and can leak under shingles. Our inspections evaluate attic insulation, ventilation and eave details — the root causes of ice dams — so you can correct them before winter.

My roof is leaking. Can you find the source?

Yes. Leaks often appear far from where water actually enters the roof. We trace leak paths using visual inspection, moisture meters and, when helpful, infrared thermal imaging to locate the true source — whether it's a failed pipe boot, flashing, valley, skylight or damaged shingles.
