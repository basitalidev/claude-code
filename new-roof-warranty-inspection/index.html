Why Denver Property Owners Choose Our New Roof & Warranty Inspection

After a major Denver hailstorm, thousands of roofs are replaced in a matter of months, and installation quality varies widely. Shortcuts like improper nailing, missing starter strips, reused flashing or inadequate ventilation may not show up for years — and can void a manufacturer's warranty.

Our new roof inspections give you an independent second set of eyes. We check the visible installation against manufacturer guidelines and common code requirements so any problems can be corrected while the installer is still on the hook.

When to schedule a new roof & warranty inspection

  • Your new roof was just completed
  • You're about to make final payment to the roofer
  • You bought a home with a recently replaced roof
  • You suspect the job was rushed

What's Included

What Our New Roof & Warranty Inspection Covers

  • Shingle alignment and exposure
  • Nail placement checks (where visible)
  • Starter course and drip edge
  • Ice and water shield at eaves and valleys
  • New vs. reused flashing
  • Pipe boots and penetrations
  • Ridge vent and intake ventilation balance
  • Cleanup and debris (nails, damage to gutters)
  • Photo report of deficiencies
  • Recommendations for the installer

Before You Make Final Payment

The best time for a new roof inspection is right after installation and before final payment. If deficiencies are found, you can share the report with your contractor and have them corrected while the crew is still available.

FAQ

New Roof & Warranty Inspection FAQ

Isn't the city permit inspection enough?

Permit inspections are important but limited in scope and time. An independent inspection looks more closely at workmanship details that affect performance and warranty coverage.

What if you find installation problems?

We document each issue with photos and explanations so your contractor can correct them. Most reputable roofers appreciate clear documentation.

Can you inspect a roof that was replaced a few years ago?

Yes. Many installation defects remain visible, and we can evaluate whether the roof is performing as expected.
